# Purpose
Coordinate research and spoken writing, narration, direction, still images, animation and final verification. Each role reads its own stage resources. Preserve the creator's scope, accepted media, saved work and existing production limits.

The creator only needs to invoke this skill, request a complete video with a duration/format, and supply a short idea. Treat 'create a complete video' as authorization to produce the finished local film: develop the story/script, generate and prepare narration, direct the sequence, generate the necessary cut images and videos, assemble sound and picture, check the result and export. Use the saved theme, narrator, providers and production rules below without requiring the creator to repeat them. Infer routine creative choices and cut count from the idea and measured audio. Create a new VibeCut project for a new film, register/place its assets and deliver the actual final export with any remaining limitations. On continuation recover the existing project rather than creating another.

For this complete-video scope, stage acceptance means performing the relevant available checks and selecting assets internally so production can continue; it does not require a new human approval between script, audio, images and video. Do not ask again whether to generate the videos or stop at a script, board or production plan. This authorizes necessary generation and bounded repairs of demonstrated failures within the requested film, not unlimited creative retries. Preserve originals, receipts and actual review status; internal selection is not human approval. A creator's explicit 'script only', 'images first', review gate, budget or stop instruction narrows this scope and takes precedence. Publishing/uploading to a channel is a separate action and is not implied by local film completion. If an actual prerequisite blocks production, report the concrete blocker and preserve resumable state.

# Workflow and ownership
1. **Research and story writer:** read only [the script template](templates/script.md) for research, a compelling telling angle, clear explanation, exact spoken text and continuous audio-scene text. Camera angles, visual design, cuts and motion are downstream responsibilities. No media is authorized by a writing task.
2. **Narration:** read [audio direction](references/audio-direction.md) and [narrator settings](config/narrator-default.json). Establish the voice/delivery with a short measured pilot before a batch, respecting existing full-production authorization. Generate one continuous narration file per scene, measure it and inspect excess pauses before locking visual timing. Keep speech and performance direction separate.
3. **Director:** read [direction](references/director.md) and [the production contract](contracts/production.md). Use the full researched story, ending, exact speech, measured files/alignment and constraints to establish the persistent world, physical events, state/dependency sequence and cuts. Distinguish narration from actual recorded effects. Hand the cut/event record to the image role; camera angles and appearance are resolved there.
4. **Still-image role:** give the LLM [the complete image prompt-writer template](templates/image.md), the directed cut, narration context and references. It writes one resolved imagePrompt per cut, including camera angle, perspective, expression, appearance, framing, light and a useful single action phase. Execute that prompt with the creator's GPT image route; requested label is GPT 2.5, but record the actual callable tool/model. For demanding geometry prepare/check a cheap blockout and keep storyboard targets distinct from model controls. Register/place selected images as unapproved previews covering continuous scene audio. Pass actual selected frames and camera details forward.
5. **Shot-animation role:** give the LLM [the complete animation prompt-writer template](templates/animation.md), the directed cut, narration window and actual selected image. It writes one resolved videoPrompt per cut, including subject action, force/contact, camera movement and outgoing state. Execute that prompt with the installed MiniMax. Preserve scene audio, action order and outgoing state; align authorized effects to observed contact and place completed video.
6. **Director returns for verification and assembly:** the same director checks story, audio, images, selected videos and adjacent cuts together, assembles the film and performs the checks below. This is a workflow responsibility, not another agent or a request appended to generation prompts. Keep unavailable playback/listening judgments pending.


# Units and continuation

These resources live under `video-skills/cinematic-3d-storytelling/` in this workspace. Read only the current role's linked resources. Detail selection and measured duration govern the total cut count; do not impose the same cut quota on every scene.

A **scene** is one continuous audio unit and can contain multiple **shots/cuts**. A **shot** is one continuous camera/subject event and normally one generated video. A reverse angle or insert needs a new shot record, not a restarted sentence or a new scene audio file. Plan by meaningful spoken movements and visual information, not equal-duration blocks.

A dramatic **sequence** can span several audio scenes. An **action beat** is a causal change within or across cuts; a **keyframe** is a visual state, not automatically another cut or a timed model input. Separate these units so changing the camera does not restart speech and a long spoken passage does not force one prolonged action.

Use the creator's format and stage. Shorts may suit portrait; longer work may suit landscape. Do not turn a preference into a universal format rule. Full-production authorization can cover the workflow, while explicit review gates and stop instructions still apply. Preserve originals, creator edits, alternatives, requests, IDs and costs.

On a continuation such as 'generate videos', recover the task's current project, accepted inputs, intended cuts, existing authorization and saved job/export state before asking anything. Do not ask for subject, style or video count already established in this task. If videos already exist, identify the actual export and address the creator's requested review/correction; do not duplicate completed jobs. A stage handoff should carry the target project, ordered pending cuts and accepted inputs explicitly, rather than leaving the next agent to infer scope from a short follow-up message.

# Director verification

Audit excessive pauses on the isolated voice before locking visual timing; music/effects must not mask a delivery defect. Preserve the shared scene audio through cuts, check actual action/contact/consequence and adjacent states, align authorized effects, then assemble and inspect the film. Verify story-critical mechanical movement against its starting and required final state, and inspect the selected edit interval rather than only the full native take. At full-production scope validate a representative difficult action sequence before scaling the approach across the film.

The director performs verification after assembly and uses the same criteria at the relevant intermediate stage before scaling production. Preserve actual findings; passing a prompt or plan is not proof the output works.

At an authorized production stage, test the hardest useful short sequence containing an actual cause, decisive change and consequence, not only an easy portrait or walking shot. Review its board for dramatic development and continuity before spending on its video. Inspect resulting motion/sync at native rate when permitted; frames alone establish narrower findings. Correct a demonstrated failure at its owning stage with a bounded edit/take, preserving alternatives. Do not use an endless retry loop or multiply weak direction across a whole film. A text self-review or technically valid graph is not proof that the finished sequence is exciting.

Audit proposed coverage for physical legibility: who holds what, whether a hand or support is available, and which action the viewer must actually see. Do not crowd establishing geography, a new task, its consequence and an explanatory mechanism into a tiny interval just because they share one narration sentence. Rebalance coverage before spending on media; retain provisional timing until audio exists. Schedule notes and overlays against their actual phrase, not a later shot merely because there is room there.

Audit whether a viewer can follow the evolving event from pictures alone, where resistance or stakes change, whether decisive actions receive coverage, and whether logistics displace the promised experience. Label timings and subjective judgments honestly.

can the images communicate the event's changing situation without the narrator explaining every cause? Identify where anticipation, resistance, decisive change and consequence are visible and where routine logistics are consuming attention. Is the decisive action large and clear enough at phone size, and does the ending show a recognizable change rather than an unexplained prop? Check state resets, missing operations, geography, repeated information, factual limits, unmotivated moves and long intervals without development. Timings cover the scene audio without restarting it. This is a board assessment, not evidence of successful motion.

briefly state any material unresolved factual, narrative, language or coverage weakness. Repair defects that prevent delivery of the promise before returning the draft. Distinguish text assessment from measured speech, audience response and production results; claim none of those without evidence.

After accepted audio use [director guidance](references/director.md), then assess the actual direction artifact before image prompting. This is separate from script quality. Review the sequence's promised experience, audience concern, attempts/opposition or discoveries, decisive visible changes and payoff. Trace each essential development through beat IDs to cuts: direct depiction, deliberately offscreen or a legible ellipsis. Flag essential action displaced by support logistics, repeated neutral poses, a long interval without changing information, or camera variety that does not change the experience.

Check source/target/path/contact-or-miss/reaction, persistent states and world geography. Separate observed soundtrack anchors from narrative mentions and planned effects. Check that pose targets do not masquerade as supported generation controls. A missing decisive beat must be fixed in direction before commissioning a more detailed still. If the story itself cannot deliver its promise, identify that limitation rather than secretly inventing plot under accepted narration.

Use [image composition](templates/image.md#camera-angles-perspective-and-appearance), [image layout](templates/image.md#layout-and-blockout) and [camera motion](templates/animation.md#camera-motion-and-framing) to assess emotional framing, full-scale cues, actual camera variety, screen attention and projected source-to-target geometry. Inspect the selected picture against any computed guide: correct numbers in a prompt do not prove that the image obeys them. Diagnose whether an unintended toy/mascot tone begins in the premise and narration as well as the image. Check if a moving-object insert actually advances away from its source toward the intended target. A diagnostic defect must read at phone size; damage hidden in a scenic composition is weak coverage even when the text mentions it.

Review the board for readable physical states and dynamic composition, the video for actual action and native-rate timing, and the assembled sequence for cause/effect, rhythm, sound sync and emotional progression. Save evidence appropriate to the stage. A graph validation or self-scored board does not establish viewer excitement, successful motion or measured retention. Preserve prior plans and source audio; a bounded comparison changes direction without silently rewriting the accepted performance.

Verify critical component motion against its fixed body/support at the beginning and end of the retained edit. Separate component rotation from camera orbit, body yaw and screen-position change. Classify a partial traversal as partial even if the next cut assumes completion. Check traversal, settling and readiness for the following operation; use a short continuation or explicit state transition when authorized rather than silently certifying a missing phase. Do not claim exact angular measurement from an ambiguous stylized perspective.

Check decisive action at delivery size: can the viewer identify its origin, contact/change and consequence, or does it vanish in a distant composition, flash or occlusion? Inserts should provide useful evidence, not replay another shot's operation. Review the isolated narration for excess pauses before declaring brisk delivery; a clean mixed waveform can hide dead air under effects. Keep a detected gap, its diagnosed source, an actual correction and a listening judgment as separate findings.

Check the ending's visual meaning against its actual spoken promise: survival, a change in control, a mechanism's failure or a discovery must be readable from established context. A decorative or invented mechanism does not establish a technical state merely because its prompt names one. Inspect the actual ending at viewing size and its join from the preceding cut; preserve uncertainty rather than certifying mechanical accuracy from an arbitrary image. Review decisive action/result frames as well as opening inputs so the production board cannot hide missing contact or consequence.

After a scene's images are selected and registered, place them immediately as held unapproved previews with phrase-based cut points. Preserve originals and alternatives. Once video is completed, check opening/middle/end geometry, action/contact/consequence, persistent state and adjacent joins. Inspect at native rate when available before claiming natural timing or force. Frame sheets and probes support narrower technical/visual findings only. Keep unavailable listening/playback approval pending. Save actual paid calls once, credits separately from unknown GCP infrastructure cost, and allow the installed shared idle coordinator to unload model weights after all submitted jobs finish.

# Production boundaries
Higgsfield handles audio, GPT handles still images, and the creator's installed MiniMax handles video. Do not substitute Higgsfield video or Qwen images. Use [the creator's saved Holden/Seed Audio default](config/narrator-default.json) unless overridden, retaining the exact voice pair and nonzero pace settings across scenes and projects. Discover actual model controls and do not invent emotion fields. Audio direction must request expressive communication and scene-specific changes using the actual model's documented prompting mode. The audio reference includes faster-speech and conservative dead-air editing guidance; a speed edit cannot create acting.

For MiniMax read `tools/minimax_h3/AI_INSTRUCTIONS.md`, use the existing client/live configuration and documented private IAP route, and reconcile saved jobs before submission. The shared GPU is sequential and automatically unloads weights after all submitted work is finished. Do not alter services, GPU stack, IAM or networking as routine production.

Report what the evidence establishes. Text reviews are not listening tests or audience metrics. Frames and probes do not establish native-rate motion. Preserve pending playback checks and unpriced infrastructure costs honestly. No media retry batch follows a writing comparison automatically.
