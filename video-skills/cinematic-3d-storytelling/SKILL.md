# Purpose
Turn a brief into something worth listening to and later watching. Learn transferable craft: a developing situation or line of thought, selective concrete detail, clear information flow, local anticipation, fitting voice and an earned ending. Choose the form for this subject and audience instead of imposing one plot, genre, duration or narrator persona.

For every new script read [the story engine and reusable writer prompt](references/story-engine.md) and [the scene/output contract](references/output-contract.md). These resources live under `video-skills/cinematic-3d-storytelling/` in this workspace. The prompt accepts one paragraph. Infer routine creative choices; when duration is absent, choose and state a fitting scale. Short work needs selection; long work needs development, not padding.

For creator-requested review or automatic template refinement, read [the independent review and refinement workflow](references/quality-review.md). Keep reference examples out of fresh writer contexts and preserve failed tests.

# Workflow and units
1. Write the whole idea, progression, ending and exact spoken script. Revise it as speech before planning coverage. Then divide it into audio scenes with proposed shots. No media is authorized by a writing task.
2. When the creator authorizes audio, read [audio direction](references/audio-direction.md). Establish the voice/delivery with a short measured pilot before a batch. After acceptance generate all scene narration, one continuous file per scene, and measure it. Audit excessive pauses on the isolated voice before locking visual timing; music/effects must not mask a delivery defect. Script instructions and spoken words remain separate.
3. After audio acceptance under the requested scope, read [the audio-to-visual director](references/action-direction.md). Hand off the whole idea and ending, exact speech, measured files/alignment and story constraints. First save the world, audience concern, physical action/dependency plan and essential visible developments. Then direct cuts. Writer-proposed shots remain provisional; do not turn them straight into a batch of image prompts. Distinguish narration-only audio from an action soundtrack and distinguish observed sound from planned effects.
4. Read [camera and layout](references/camera-and-layout.md), then [the image/video templates](references/visual-prompts.md). Resolve viewpoint intent, full-scale staging, source-to-target geometry and the camera's attachment/look-at/focus separately. For geometry-sensitive action prepare a simple blockout and check its projected relations before paid images. Build each image from its directed cut and opening state, with scoped layout/cast references; inspect the actual result against the guide. Prepare useful critical/outgoing key states as needed, keeping storyboard guides distinct from model controls. Use the creator's GPT image route; requested label is GPT 2.5, but record the actual tool/model rather than guessing an alias. Register and place selected images as unapproved previews covering the continuous scene audio.
5. After image acceptance under the requested scope, generate each continuous shot with the creator's installed MiniMax on GCP. Preserve the shared scene audio through cuts, check actual action/contact/consequence and adjacent states, align authorized effects, then assemble and inspect the film. Verify story-critical mechanical movement against its starting and required final state, and inspect the selected edit interval rather than only the full native take. At full-production scope validate a representative difficult action sequence before scaling the approach across the film.

A **scene** is one continuous audio unit and can contain multiple **shots/cuts**. A **shot** is one continuous camera/subject event and normally one generated video. A reverse angle or insert needs a new shot record, not a restarted sentence or a new scene audio file. Plan by meaningful spoken movements and visual information, not equal-duration blocks.

A dramatic **sequence** can span several audio scenes. An **action beat** is a causal change within or across cuts; a **keyframe** is a visual state, not automatically another cut or a timed model input. Separate these units so changing the camera does not restart speech and a long spoken passage does not force one prolonged action.

Use the creator's format and stage. Shorts may suit portrait; longer work may suit landscape. Do not turn a preference into a universal format rule. Full-production authorization can cover the workflow, while explicit review gates and stop instructions still apply. Preserve originals, creator edits, alternatives, requests, IDs and costs.

On a continuation such as 'generate videos', recover the task's current project, accepted inputs, intended cuts, existing authorization and saved job/export state before asking anything. Do not ask for subject, style or video count already established in this task. If videos already exist, identify the actual export and address the creator's requested review/correction; do not duplicate completed jobs. A stage handoff should carry the target project, ordered pending cuts and accepted inputs explicitly, rather than leaving the next agent to infer scope from a short follow-up message.

# Story and voice
Choose the telling angle before drafting: the listener's entry moment, viewpoint or narrator relationship, and reason to keep listening. For this creator's conversational YouTube style, begin communicating an unfolding event or interesting claim to the audience. Avoid spending the opening inviting them into an exercise or reading the premise aloud. Context should help them follow; the developing angle should carry through the explanation. Use the story engine for the decision criteria, without imposing a cold open or fictional character on every subject.

Let the listener follow change. For a story, particular actions and responses carry it; for an explanation, observations and relationships build understanding; for an investigation, evidence changes an account. Combine forms where useful. Do not force every topic into a disaster, a mystery or a character quest.

Give concrete material room to work. When an event already makes its consequence clear, continue; explanation should add a relationship the listener cannot infer. Avoid turning every beat into a lesson about why it matters. Let personality emerge through what the narrator notices, how thoughts connect and where emphasis belongs, rather than through stock hooks, fake mistakes or borrowed mannerisms.

Select details that let the listener reconstruct the event: what someone is trying to do, the object or constraint involved, the decisive physical operation and what changes because of it. Give a mechanism, revealing behavior or consequential material detail enough specificity to be pictured. Do not replace those with generic drama, or invent facts and private thoughts to make a reconstruction more cinematic. Delivery should be brisk and emotionally engaged by default for this creator's YouTube work, with changes driven by meaning rather than a uniformly slow dramatic read. Preserve connected speech and comprehension.

Write connected spoken sentences: keep related actions and their causes or consequences together, preserve clear agency through names and pronouns, and use a short sentence when emphasis earns the pause. Scene and shot divisions do not dictate sentence breaks. Choose whether to lead with the action or result and contextualize afterward; do not force suspense by interrupting a small incident and replaying it later. Review the uninterrupted speech as well as its scene records.

Keep fact, reconstruction, fiction and counterfactual allowances distinct in production metadata and research notes. For this creator's story channel, narration stays inside the unfolding story: do not interrupt it with 'this is fictional', 'unrealistic', 'imagine', 'let us assume', 'we can hypothesize', or equivalent production disclaimers and invitations to an exercise. Do not put those interruptions into captions either. Let the events establish the premise; preserve its internal rules without explaining the act of inventing it. Do not claim invented events are verified history or current news. Research load-bearing factual claims for factual work; keep sources and verification needs in notes unless examining evidence is the requested story itself.

# Visual theme
The creator's default is **detailed low-poly Blender-style cinematic 3D**: deliberately simplified modeled forms and readable faceted silhouettes, consistent stylized people and props, tangible restrained material response, believable contact shadows, depth, atmospheric light and coherent set geometry. Keep richness in composition, lighting, staging and meaningful modeled detail; do not drift into photographic humans, glossy toy figures, flat illustration or generic realistic CGI. Slight exaggeration may improve clarity, expression or force while support, contact and object persistence stay readable. Carry this explicit look through both image and video prompts unless the creator overrides it. A Blender-like look does not prove that an actual Blender scene was built.

Low-poly describes modeling language, not miniature scale or a playful persona. For grounded action, retain full-scale machinery, convincing construction and material response, environmental scale cues and a tone suited to the stakes. Choose comedy, toy proportions or anthropomorphic behavior when the brief supports them. Decide whose experience each camera serves: an empowering low view and an oppressive low view can share an angle while having different staging, light and behavior. Do not obtain 'drama' by applying threat framing to every participant.

Build scenes as events covered by several purposeful virtual camera positions. Show preparation, action and consequence where understanding depends on them; choose wide geography, contact detail, tracking, reaction or reverse views for the information they reveal. Each selected still is the usable opening of one later continuous video, not a whole scene summarized in one pose. Keep the same environment and cast across those views. Derive the number of cuts from the event and measured audio, never from the current film's 42-cut count. Use the linked visual planner to resolve coverage before spending on images.

Continuity is necessary but does not establish compelling direction. Make the audience experience an objective meeting resistance, a discovery or a change in risk, knowledge or options. Give the story's decisive physical developments visible coverage; assess whether routine logistics are displacing the promised experience. More images, more camera moves and more explosions are not substitutes for that progression. Preserve the story's factual/fictional boundaries and accepted outcome when elaborating its visuals.

# Production boundaries
Higgsfield handles audio, GPT handles still images, and the creator's installed MiniMax handles video. Do not substitute Higgsfield video or Qwen images. Use [the creator's saved Holden/Seed Audio default](references/narrator-default.json) unless overridden, retaining the exact voice pair and nonzero pace settings across scenes and projects. Discover actual model controls and do not invent emotion fields. Audio direction must request expressive communication and scene-specific changes using the actual model's documented prompting mode. The audio reference includes faster-speech and conservative dead-air editing guidance; a speed edit cannot create acting.

For MiniMax read `tools/minimax_h3/AI_INSTRUCTIONS.md`, use the existing client/live configuration and documented private IAP route, and reconcile saved jobs before submission. The shared GPU is sequential and automatically unloads weights after all submitted work is finished. Do not alter services, GPU stack, IAM or networking as routine production.

Report what the evidence establishes. Text reviews are not listening tests or audience metrics. Frames and probes do not establish native-rate motion. Preserve pending playback checks and unpriced infrastructure costs honestly. No media retry batch follows a writing comparison automatically.
