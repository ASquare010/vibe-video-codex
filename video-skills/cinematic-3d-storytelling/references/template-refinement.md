# Text review and template refinement

Use when the creator asks for script review, comparisons or automatic prompt refinement. It is a writing workflow; do not turn it into paid media generation. A model judgment is editorial evidence, not proof of audience response.

## Independent review prompt
```text
Read the creator brief and the complete spoken script before its production notes. Judge the experience this particular subject and format calls for. Do not impose one genre, narrator persona, hook formula or plot structure.

BRIEF
{ONE_PARAGRAPH_BRIEF}

DRAFT
{COMPLETE_IDEA_FULL_SPOKEN_TEXT_AND_SCENE_RECORDS}

Assess:
- What does the opening actually begin delivering, and what makes the next development worth hearing?
- Without the title, visuals or performance notes, does the opening already communicate an interesting event, observation or claim to a listener? Identify the actual telling angle and audience relationship, rather than crediting a concrete prop or present tense alone. Does the following movement develop it, or retreat into introductory framing and a sequence of topic headings?
- Can a listener follow a developing situation or line of thought? Where does it stall or become a list?
- Which concrete details add useful information? Which explanations supply a missing relationship, and which merely restate the obvious?
- Do sentence connections, referents, rhythm and attitude work as spoken thought?
- Read across punctuation and scene boundaries: where do names echo, subjects reset mechanically, or connected actions get split into tiny statements? Which sentences should carry a cause, condition or consequence together? Flag ambiguous pronouns as well as excessive name repetition; longer is not automatically smoother.
- Does the chosen story order earn its interruptions? Could the defining action or result arrive earlier with context afterward? Identify a suspended incident, rewind, recap or concluding paraphrase that merely repeats a known state without adding useful understanding. Do not impose result-first or chronological order on every story.
- Does it sound addressed to someone, with meaningful noticing, response and emphasis, or like an article being read? Distinguish useful explanation from a narrator announcing the exercise or describing how the video will proceed. Do not prescribe slang, staccato sentences or a particular opening word as the cure.
- For this creator's story channel, flag any interruption declaring fiction, unrealistic conditions, imagination, assumptions or hypothetical setup to the audience. Keep classification and production qualifications out of speech and overlays; check the whole script, not only its opening. Preserve factual integrity in metadata and do not turn an invention into a claim of verified history or news.
- Does the requested length buy development? In longer work, do subquestions connect and deepen rather than restart?
- Does the ending fulfill the real promise and honor the script's established commitments? Check relevant people, resources, rules, time, quantities and broad group claims against their actual last-known states.
- Are factual claims and invention distinguishable, without invented evidence or distracting sourcing commentary?
- Does the scene text preserve the whole script exactly? Can a first-time listener identify each necessary causal or explanatory connection in the actual words? Shot planning is the director's later responsibility and is not a writer deliverable.

Return a compact verdict and concrete findings with exact short quotations, severity, what already works, and the function a repair must achieve. Do not supply an ideal replacement plot unless asked. Separate actual defects from taste. Distinguish story time, estimated screen time and unmeasured delivery. Never infer voice quality, retention, audience approval or media success from this text.
```

## Refining the reusable prompt
Use a fresh writer context containing only the candidate rules and an ordinary brief. Do not send source examples, past answers, expected solutions or grader preferences to that writer. A reviewer may inspect references for transferable craft, keeping reference exposure explicit.

Diagnose the observed failure before changing rules. Change a generative decision, remove a misleading instruction or clarify a real contract; do not append a ban for every weak phrase. Keep plot-specific repairs in the project draft. The reusable prompt must not accumulate source topics, facts, example scripts, signature jokes or ideal endings.

Preserve frozen versions and raw failures. Compare changes on identical briefs when attributing effects, then test unfamiliar subjects and both short and long formats. A brief change is a separate variable. Keep less favorable reviews and remaining weaknesses visible. Once the engine transfers usefully, stop chasing one reviewer's score and let future use reveal new limitations. An exact-span overlap scan supports a narrow copying check; it does not prove originality or equal creative quality.

Use available independent agents only within the creator's authorization. When they are unavailable, perform a clearly labeled self-review rather than invent an independent evaluation.

For a requested fresh-conversation comparison, pass the same minimal brief and the candidate skill, excluding prior answers, ideal shot lists and project-specific fixes. For a visual-only comparison, freeze the accepted narration file/text/timing, format and provider settings where available. A clean input package may contain those raw inputs without exposing previous image/video prompts or outputs. Record changed variables and tool randomness; newly generated assets make this a practical comparison, not a controlled proof of prompt quality. Preserve the baseline project and create the new production separately.

This resource is for explicitly requested script/template review and comparisons. It is not an additional automatic film-review role. The director's ordinary final verification is routed by SKILL.md.
