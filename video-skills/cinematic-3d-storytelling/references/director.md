# Director: events, coverage and timing

Use after the researched story and narration are accepted. Own the persistent world, physical events, cut membership, action-state continuity and editorial timing. The image prompt writer chooses concrete composition/angle/appearance; the animation prompt writer resolves motion and camera travel. Keep their returned details in the shared record. The same director returns for verification through SKILL.md; this guide does not create another review agent.

## Inputs and evidence

Take the whole story and actual ending, exact speech, selected audio paths/version IDs/hashes, measured duration, available alignment, factual/fictional limits and accepted cast/prop/set references. Preserve selected speech and media. Existing writer shot suggestions are provisional; new writers return a story rather than a camera list. If the story lacks a necessary development, return that defect to writing instead of secretly inventing an event behind accepted narration.

Identify narration-only, supplied action soundtrack or hybrid audio. Speech mentions are semantic windows, not recorded impacts. For supplied effects, record the actual sound file, interval, time base and evidence separately from inferred causes. Keep effects that will be added later labelled planned. Use available alignment/analysis tools as needed; do not fabricate timestamps, confidence or sound events, or install an analysis stack as a routine prerequisite.

## Direct what changes and what the audience needs

Choose the sequence's participant objective or line of inquiry, audience concern, obstacle/opposing response and changing situation. An explanation may develop a discovery rather than an adversary. Quiet behavior can carry a revelation. Let the accepted form govern intensity and scale; low-poly styling does not require toys, comedy or miniature worlds.

Choose the essential visible facts and their causal dependencies: what prepares an action, the action/contact or miss, response, persistent result and consequent options. Specify the meaningful event phase as well as the subject: an approach, an operation already underway and its aftermath permit different actions. Include scale, location or stage when they distinguish the narrated event from a merely related picture. An indispensable short operation deserves coverage even if it lasts less than a second. Do not substitute walking/loading/greeting logistics for the promised encounter, nor add gratuitous destruction to create an action quota. Preserve what participants know, where they are, what they hold, support, damage and capabilities.

For each beat state its audience effect and new information: dominance, vulnerability, anticipation, discovery, force, surprise or another relevant intention. This tells the image writer why a view matters without prescribing a lens or angle here. A sequence may cross continuous audio scenes. Follow the developing event rather than illustrating each spoken noun.

For a load-bearing change, save a visible evidence target in the shared direction/review record: the distinguishing before state, the operation or discovery, and the observable result that permits the next event. Name what the viewer could actually notice, not just labels such as "recognizes," "repaired" or "safe." Contextual views can accompany narration; identify which view or sequence carries the essential evidence. An abstract explanation may use a supported explanatory graphic or comparison instead of pretending invisible behavior was photographed. Keep inferred or elided completion distinct from a continuously demonstrated action.

## Coverage and uneven durations

Each scene contains at least three meaningful cuts for this creator's workflow; the total grows with useful coverage. Choose scene boundaries and script scope before recording so a scene can support that coverage. If frozen audio makes the requirement infeasible, expose that constraint rather than padding, accelerating speech or silently rewriting it. Neither the minimum nor the previous film's 42 cuts is an equal-length recipe.

Allocate time by what must be perceived and felt. Geography and an approaching major action may need the longest view; a short contact insert can carry a decisive fact; a reaction or reveal may need its own beat. "Short" is relative to the motion, readability and dramatic purpose: it has no fixed one-second target or fixed share of the scene. Do not remove the insert merely to reach a preferred minimum shot length. Equally, do not make every verb another shot. Separate cuts where a new view, attention target or phase makes the event clearer or more expressive.

Use these functions when they serve the scene, not as a mandatory sequence:
- Establish/re-establish where participants, routes and important objects are.
- Lead or prepare attention through an eyeline, approach, gesture, sound or expectant frame.
- Isolate a causal detail or contact the wide view would hide.
- Continue one action across a cut with matching phase, direction, ownership and momentum.
- Show its consequence, reaction or reveal once the needed setup exists.

A lead-in can prepare the viewer before the narrated mention. A cutaway or reaction can accompany explanatory narration. Record that relationship explicitly and preserve the event's truth: context is allowed; an opposite action or impossible state is a defect. An ellipsis can omit irrelevant time but must not quietly certify an unseen critical operation.

## Time the board before scaling video

After measured speech, make a timed coverage/animatic pass using lightweight placeholders or the available stills, then inspect the selected-image board before batch animation. Save cut boundaries, the facts each cut must communicate and intended phase windows. The timing pass is a project artifact, not a second media-generation request or a requirement to install animation software. If playback is unavailable, save a provisional frame table and keep the felt-rhythm judgment pending.

Compare actual adjacent stills without relying on their titles. Distinct planned intentions can collapse into the same selected composition or task. Keep a repeated view when changed behavior or information earns it; otherwise redirect its job or consolidate coverage while respecting the scene contract. Ask the image role for the view that exposes the missing relationship, rather than prescribing a compulsory angle rotation. A static hardware or environment insert earns a long interval through useful discovery, spatial understanding or suspense; decorative camera travel alone does not supply a missing explanation.

There are three distinct durations: native model output, useful source action, and selected edit. A provider's generation handle does not set the final cut's duration. Prepare useful beginning/contact/end storyboard states where needed; a panel is not automatically a new editorial cut or a supported timed model control. Prefer one compatible selected opening image for a continuous generated take. Never invent start/end/pose/depth API controls.

Choose action-specific rhythm: readable anticipation, decisive acceleration/contact, then a result or recovery. Smooth camera travel does not imply slow action. Intentionally slow suspense and brief time-expanded fast-object inserts may be appropriate; label them separately from accidental floaty cadence. More useful cuts can cover speech without stretching a quick action. Preserve action count across angles: a release, travel view and impact can be one event, not three repeated operations.

After generation, select the complete useful source interval and trim idle handles. When needed, use a justified playback adjustment within actual editor limits, preserving contact, support, result and camera readability. Save source in/out, final duration, speed and the reason; preserve originals and unchanged narration. Do not use retiming to disguise the wrong action, missing contact or a broken handoff. Align effects to the final observed contact after selection/retiming.

Judge preparation, action and result across the sequence, not each cut in isolation. A useful lead-in should not become repeated hovering or a fresh reach in every view. Budget readable movement and exposed result after any preceding preparation; choose the duration from this motion and audience need, without a fixed fraction for each phase. Compare the actual before/result views at delivery viewing size: a caption or sound may reinforce a crucial change but cannot certify a missing operation. If the target remains unclear, record that finding and correct the owning input or selection within the authorized repair scope; do not accept the prompt's intended state as observed completion.

For narration-only work there is no frame-exact sound anchor until a real sound is supplied or placed. For supplied effects, plan preparation before the measured contact, allowing a deliberate documented offset. Keep scene-local, cut-local, native-source and final-edit time bases distinct. Match the final selected interval to the same narrative event, not merely the native take's best-looking moment.

## One shared world and adjacent-cut packet

Keep stable actor/prop/set IDs, dimensions, positions/facing, routes/exits, supports, sightlines, lighting, ownership, condition and capabilities. World locations remain fixed across viewpoint changes; screen left/right is camera-relative. Use accepted references first and scope design references to their actual cast/prop/location role. The image role resolves difficult projected geometry/blockout; a written or computed plan does not prove the generated frame obeys it.

Give both prompt-writing LLMs the same versioned record:
- Previous: actual selected outgoing state/reference when available, otherwise planned state explicitly labelled; motion phase, velocity/direction, held objects and screen attention.
- Current: stable scene/cut and beat/dependency IDs, exact narration window, visual development/audience effect, incoming state, opening action phase, intended operation and outgoing state.
- Next: required incoming state and whether the join continues action, reacts, changes location or elides time. The next event is editorial context only.
- Timing: frame interval against measured audio, action phase windows, intended time treatment, native capability constraints separately, and actual source selection/rate when available.

The LLM uses adjacent context to preserve the join, then compiles only the current event into the provider request. Do not leak a future prop, arrest, location or action into the current take. If the selected image cannot perform its directed operation, return the discrepancy to its owning stage rather than silently redefining the cut.

## Records, scope and completion evidence

Save the source/sequence/world/audioEvents/beats/cuts/review artifact using [the production contract](../contracts/production.md). Keep complete prompts, actual references/hashes, submitted requests, IDs, alternatives, costs and findings. This artifact complements ProjectStore; do not overwrite project.json with portable planning JSON or revise historical submissions after a template update.

For critical mechanics record the moving component, fixed body/reference, start/end relation, joint/path and extent, completion window and visible endpoint. Camera orbit, body movement and component travel are different. Record actual selected-range completion as not-checked, complete, partial, elided or unclear. A subsequent operation begins from the achieved state. A symbolic ending or unfamiliar mechanism needs observable context and outcome, not an invented interface labelled as proof.

Writing/direction alone authorizes no media. Full-film production authorization can cover these stages; respect any narrower creator gate, budget or stop. Final verification belongs to the director through SKILL.md, including actual audio/visual agreement and native-rate checks when available.

## Research basis and adaptation

[Brooklyn College's film glossary](https://userhome.brooklyn.cuny.edu/anthro/jbeatty/COURSES/glossary.htm) describes inserts as informational/emphatic detail and matching action across an edit. [Animation Mentor's staging guidance](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/staging-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/) connects viewer attention with composition and screen direction. [Its timing/spacing tutorial](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-animate-with-timing-and-spacing-in-mind/) demonstrates timing contrast, anticipation and retiming. [Toon Boom's animatic curriculum](https://learn.toonboom.com/modules/animatic) treats panel duration, camera and sound as editable parts of the animatic.

The staged-LLM packet, minimum cut count and provider-selection procedure are our adaptation, not guarantees or numerical rules from those sources. No source prescribes one universal shot duration.
