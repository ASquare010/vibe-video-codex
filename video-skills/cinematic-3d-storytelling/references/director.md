# Director: treatment, timing and the viewing experience

The director owns staging, cuts, performance direction, shared world continuity and final verification. The writer owns the story and exact narration. Image writing resolves concrete composition/angle/appearance; animation writing resolves physical action and camera travel. Give them a clear creative job without writing their technical prompts here.

## Before narration: direct the whole story
Read the full writer's story, exact narration, ending, factual limits, brief and available references as read-only input, even before audio exists. Decide how staging and performance let the audience anticipate, discover and feel its developments. Do not rewrite, shorten, reorder or paraphrase the spoken text, change causes or outcomes, or add plot events. Return unclear explanation, unsupported claims or a story that cannot be staged coherently to the writer with the specific problem. Resume direction from the writer's revised version; do not repair story logic inside visual prompts or production notes.

Plan visible developments alongside the unchanged words. Narration need not name every movement. Establishment, approaches, urgent reactions, a loaded pause or the consequence of an action can carry a moment while speech leads, follows or briefly stops. Such staging realizes the existing event without changing who acts, what happens or why. For factual reconstruction, distinguish supported events from dramatized behavior in the production notes; do not invent evidence or private dialogue.

Choose provisional cuts by the information and experience they add. A scale-setting view can build anticipation, a short detail can supply a decisive cause, and a reaction/reveal can show what it changes. Make the actual event develop; repeated people inspecting something is not automatically a sequence. For an explanation, develop a relationship, not merely a succession of related objects.

Name what the audience should notice first in each important cut. Keep the spoken promise, visual development and sound directed toward the same experience; they need not describe the same instant. Let changing conditions renew anticipation. Do not use a fixed cut frequency, compulsory overlay or repeated verbal teaser in place of development. Leave angle, framing and optical choices to the image role.

For every important beat establish:
- The objective/question, pressure or obstacle and the new information.
- Incoming phase and shared geography, intended action/discovery, outgoing state and what it enables next.
- A visible before/change/result cue for a load-bearing development. Keep inferred, elided and continuously demonstrated completion distinct.
- The spoken passage, delivery intention, meaningful non-spoken space and sound intention.

Return the unchanged story and narration plus directed scenes/cuts using [the record contract](../contracts/production.md). In managed VibeCut return complete ordered scenes with their existing IDs, unchanged plan.story/fullNarration and scene narration, plus direction, spokenStyle and at least three provisional cuts per scene, including two brief cuts as described below. Each cut has a purpose, exact narrationAnchor and startState/endState; its frame values remain provisional. Camera prompts may stay empty. Performance intentions stay outside narration. Current recorded speech and its delivery are locked unless their revision is authorized.

## After narration: finalize timing
Use the selected audio version/path/hash, measured duration, available alignment and any pause-edit map. Finalize cut intervals against that file, preserving writer words, cut identity/order, purpose and event states. In managed VibeCut the app stamps the project treatment basis; scene timing results add directionBasis and change frame intervals, not the treatment. If an actual script/event change becomes necessary, return it to the writer, then deliberately update dependent direction/audio rather than hiding it in visual prompts.

Each scene needs at least three meaningful cuts, including at least two brief cuts. Plan these before recording, then finalize their actual duration against measured audio and the complete useful movement. Usually aim around 0.5–1.5 seconds for the brief cuts; this is an approximate range, not a fixed one-second shot or a reason to truncate a movement that needs nearer two seconds. A hand securing a key, a latch giving way, a newly noticed clue or the immediate result of an action can make the next event understandable. A generic shocked face or duplicate view does not satisfy that purpose by itself.

Give establishment or a major continuous movement the longer coverage it needs, then use the brief cuts to carry preparation, decisive detail and consequence between developments. Do not limit a scene to three cuts, make every verb a cut, insert short shots at a fixed frequency or merely split one long hold to meet the count. Narration continues across cuts; it does not need a sentence or audio file for each insert. If a proposed insert is unreadable, improve the staging or allow it more time while retaining two useful brief cuts elsewhere in the scene. Raise a genuine timing conflict rather than silently dropping coverage or changing the story.

The video model's minimum native duration does not determine the cut's edit duration. Select a complete useful movement from a longer native take; retime only when the motion supports it, preserving momentum, contact and result. Do not slow a quick action to fill the audio or speed up narration to fit the pictures.

Evaluate preparation, movement and result across the whole sequence. Carry a reach into contact instead of restarting it in each view. Reserve time for the actual operation and an exposed result after the preceding setup. Suspenseful holds earn their duration through expectation; incidental empty waits do not. A timed board/animatic is provisional if playback is unavailable.

## One world, useful handoffs
Keep stable cast/prop/set IDs, scale, positions/facing, routes, supports, ownership, condition, knowledge and light. Match the meaningful event phase, not only its subject name. World locations remain fixed across angles; screen directions are camera-relative.

For connected objects and consequential movement, establish the shared arrangement once: what connects to what, which surfaces face each other, the route and fixed landmarks, and how the actor can reach the next required location or control. Identify unresolved physical assumptions before requesting polished views. The image role turns this world into concrete camera/layout and pose references; a character reference alone cannot establish the set or mechanism.

Give each prompt writer the same compact packet:
- Previous actual outgoing frame/state, or explicitly planned state if not generated, and the action/object/eyeline/location/sound it passes forward.
- Current cut/beat ID, speech window, first-notice priority, new information, opening phase, decisive action/change and required outgoing state.
- Next required incoming state and join type: continuation, reaction, new location or deliberate ellipsis; identify what lets the viewer connect the two.
- Selected duration and intended action phases; actual references, source range/rate and evidence status when available.

Neighboring events inform the writers, but only the current take belongs in the provider prompt. Missing coverage returns to the director; a wrong opening image returns to the image stage. An abstract mechanism may need a supported explanatory view/graphic rather than invented hardware behavior.

Choose how a join works: a continued action, matching eyeline/direction, a newly revealed relation, sound carried across the cut, or an explicit change of time/place. Exact end-frame matching is useful only when continuity requires it. A reaction or insert can bridge an edit, but cannot certify a missing essential action. Keep start/key-action/edit-out targets as planning evidence for difficult shots; they are not automatically extra generated images or supported video controls.

## Selection and assembly
Compare actual adjacent stills without their descriptive titles. Keep repeated composition when changed content earns it; otherwise redirect/consolidate coverage or ask the image role to expose the missing relation. A new lens number alone is not a new idea. Validate critical geometry with the available blockout tools when needed.

Compare actual before/result cues at delivery viewing size. Inspect the complete useful movement before choosing source in/out; trim idle handles, then consider justified retiming while preserving contact, support, momentum and result. Keep native output, source selection and edit duration separate. When trimming or retiming a shot, apply the same source-time mapping to its synchronized effects/captions/overlays; keep the accepted narration on its own continuous clock. Check the edited shot in isolation and in the assembled sequence, including sound tails. Mix ambience/physical sound beneath narration with intentional quiet where useful. A caption, click or flash cannot certify an obscured operation.

Distinguish narration mentions from measured sound effects. Do not invent word/impact timestamps or listening findings. Return for the actual-output and export checks in SKILL.md; preserve partial or unreviewed findings honestly.
