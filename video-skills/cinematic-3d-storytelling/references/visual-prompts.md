# Low-poly Blender-style image and video direction

Read after the story and scene audio are accepted and [the audio-to-visual director](action-direction.md) has produced the sequence/world/beat plan. Image prompts are compiled from directed cuts and their physical states, using the whole story and exact audio as context. Do not skip choreography by translating each spoken noun or writer-proposed shot directly into an image. Resolve templates into concise standalone instructions; omit irrelevant clauses rather than stacking every module onto every shot.

Read [camera and layout](camera-and-layout.md) before resolving the board. For demanding geometry include a checked composition/blocking guide; preserve its projected relationships through image development. A good style match is insufficient when the selected still has a wrong viewpoint, unconvincing scale or a source aimed away from its intended target.

The low-poly look below is this creator's saved default. An explicit style override replaces the whole default look clause in the resolved prompts; do not append a conflicting aesthetic to it. Planning fields, reference roles and cut timing are production records, not unsupported provider parameters.

## Shared world contract
Define the rendering language, subject design, scale, materials, lighting, set geometry and recurring landmarks once. The creator's default is detailed low-poly Blender-style cinematic 3D: intentional simplified geometry, legible faceted silhouettes, consistent stylized anatomy, restrained physically based materials, contact shadows, atmospheric depth and motivated light. Detail comes from staging, modeled construction, selective surface wear and lighting; it does not mean increasing polygon density until the look becomes photoreal. Maintain readable silhouettes and subject/background separation. Bodies, props, creatures and backgrounds share this rendering language. Avoid photographic heads on stylized bodies, plastic display figurines, flat illustrations or frozen anime tableaux unless explicitly intended. Repeat the resolved theme in every standalone image and video prompt; a style label saved only in planning notes will not reach the model.

Slight exaggeration can improve pose readability, acceleration, force, scale and emotional expression. Preserve support, contact and persistent objects. Detail and realism do not require mechanically exact documentary simulation. A requested Blender-like look is an aesthetic; do not claim an actual Blender scene or rendered camera rig exists unless one was built.

Default to full-scale subjects within this stylized language. State the physical scale and construction cues, camera distance/height relative to the subject, and the intended tonal register. A tiny or mascot-like protagonist, toy proportions, a miniature camera treatment and playful direction can overpower a negative clause saying 'no toys'. Preserve deliberate miniature sequences only when the story actually calls for that scale.

For connected coverage retain world-space relationships, ownership and causal state. Screen coordinates may change with camera travel. Establish the relevant geography before a reverse angle makes it ambiguous. A new view is a new view of the same set, not a new set arranged to resemble the old picture.

## Visual coverage planner — run before the image batch

The image planner turns the director's reviewed beat plan into a filmable board, not a set of scene-summary illustrations. Read the sequence objective, audience concern, causal dependencies, exact accepted scene audio and established world. Find the strongest visible way to communicate the current development. A spectacular view earns its place through scale, discovery, risk, behavior or a decisive physical change. A quiet attempt to avoid notice can need closer observation and partial concealment; a fast mechanism can need an unobstructed contact detail and a camera tied to its output. Do not apply the same orbit, slow push or level of agitation to both.

Map the event's necessary phases before choosing images: access or arrival, approach or preparation, contact, operation or transfer, result and continuation as applicable. Give a separate cut to a phase when its detail or new viewpoint is needed to understand the event; combine phases when one continuous view can show them clearly. Skip redundant phases and use deliberate ellipses for unimportant travel. The current film's cut count and particular sequence are not a universal shot recipe.

Treat connected views as cameras placed around one persistent set. Record a simple spatial layout: entrances, important objects, paths, supports, light source and recognizable landmarks. Keep a stable character/prop design record and evolving state record. For each cut resolve who is present, who is offscreen, where the object is, who supports it and what has already happened. Distinguish a harmless screen-position change from a contradictory world-position change. Preserve movement direction and eyelines; if crossing the action axis or changing direction, establish the change visibly instead of teleporting subjects or mirroring the room.

Choose the camera by what the audience needs next:
- Establish geography and the route before a close view would make the action confusing.
- Move alongside, lead or follow a person/object when their progress carries the event. An attached viewpoint moves with its subject while the fixed environment produces coherent parallax.
- Use a contact insert when a hand, tool, support or mechanism explains the change; keep its decisive contact readable.
- Arc, pan or rise when this reveals previously hidden information, scale or a reaction. A camera turning in place and a camera translating through space are different instructions.
- Use a reverse or reaction angle when the action changes what another participant sees, knows or does. Preserve geography and agency.

Favor visible subject activity plus a purposeful camera move over repeated drifting past held poses. One smooth move does not mean slow action: the camera can follow fast action continuously at natural speed. Let camera inertia, subject acceleration, foreground crossings and depth create energy; do not use zoom, blur, orbit or shake as decoration. Avoid impossible travel through solid objects, a full orbit that must invent an unseen room, or occlusion that hides the action the cut is meant to explain. A restrained or briefly held camera is appropriate when extra movement would obscure the event.

Resolve enough coverage before paid generation to avoid discovering a missing operation during video production. Often 2–5 seconds carries one focused beat; longer shots need developing action and new information. This is a pacing guide, not an equal-block timing rule. Several phases can share a narrated clause, and narration may continue across a silent visual reaction. Protect measured audio, not the original proposed image count. If coverage needs more images, prepare those images at the authorized stage rather than asking a video model to invent several cuts inside one take.

Reusable **visual planning prompt**:
```text
Direct the visual coverage for the authorized image stage. Do not generate media from this planning request alone.
FULL IDEA AND ENDING: {COMPLETE_ACCEPTED_STORY}
SCENE AUDIO: {EXACT_SCENE_WORDS; MEASURED_DURATION; ORDERED_PHRASE_TIMES}
DIRECTOR PLAN: {SEQUENCE_OBJECTIVE_AND_AUDIENCE_CONCERN; ESSENTIAL_DEVELOPMENTS; BEAT_IDS_DEPENDENCIES_AND_BEFORE_AFTER_STATES; AUDIO_MODE_AND_EVIDENCE; REVIEWED_WORLD}
LAYOUT AND CAMERA BASIS: {SUBJECT_DIMENSIONS_POSITIONS_ORIENTATIONS; SOURCE_PATH_TARGET; CHECKED_GUIDE_OR_UNVERIFIED_STATUS; VIEWPOINT_INTENT; ATTACHMENT_LOOK_AT_FOCUS_AND_FRAMING}
ESTABLISHED WORLD: {ACCEPTED_CAST_PROP_AND_SET_REFERENCES_WITH_SCOPED_ROLES; CURRENT_STATE}
FORMAT: {ASPECT_RATIO; FPS; TARGET_IMAGE_SIZE}
LOOK: Detailed low-poly Blender-style cinematic 3D; simplified modeled forms, faceted readable silhouettes, consistent stylized cast, tangible restrained materials, contact shadows, atmospheric depth and motivated cinematic lighting. {PROJECT_SPECIFIC_PALETTE_AND_DESIGN_LOCKS_OR_EXPLICIT_CREATOR_OVERRIDE}.

If the director plan is missing, first produce it using action-direction.md before compiling prompts. Break its beats into views that make the developing action or explanation understandable and felt. Choose camera positions around the same set, carrying state forward. Develop a progression of scale, angle, visibility, attention and rhythm; do not make each narrated sentence one held illustration. Give decisive changes and their effects readable coverage instead of filling the sequence with support logistics. Choose the useful opening phase for every still so the subsequent video can perform its beat immediately. Normally one selected opening still and one continuous video per cut; additional cuts need additional records. Plan further contact/end key states only where useful, with explicit roles and generation status; do not assume that uploading them gives MiniMax timed keyframe control.

Return:
1. Shared cast/prop/set design locks and simple geography, including routes, supports, landmarks and light direction.
2. Ordered phase/coverage map identifying essential actions, causal connections, deliberate ellipses and what each view newly reveals.
3. Cut records: stable scene/shot IDs and beat/dependency IDs; exact phrase anchor and measured interval; dramatic job and audience attention; visible cast/count and offscreen participants; incoming action/ownership/state; chosen opening instant; camera start position, height, framing and attention target; one continuous path and end view; subject source/target/path/contact-or-miss/consequence and outgoing state; critical mechanism's moving part, fixed reference, traversal, endpoint and visible completion test when applicable; adjacent continuity and whether a join continues action or elides time; actual ordered reference/keyframe roles; likely shot-specific risks.
4. A resolved GPT still prompt per cut using the image template below. Keep editorial plans and movement instructions from becoming multiple times/views inside the still.
5. Coverage audit: can the images communicate the event's changing situation without the narrator explaining every cause? Identify where anticipation, resistance, decisive change and consequence are visible and where routine logistics are consuming attention. Is the decisive action large and clear enough at phone size, and does the ending show a recognizable change rather than an unexplained prop? Check state resets, missing operations, geography, repeated information, factual limits, unmotivated moves and long intervals without development. Timings cover the scene audio without restarting it. This is a board assessment, not evidence of successful motion.

Do not return a fixed cut quota. If phrase timing is unavailable, mark timings provisional and align them before placement. Do not change accepted narration to fit a convenient number of pictures. Save the plan and reference roles so the video stage can use the same motion intention and actual selected starting state.
```

```text
STORY JOB: At the spoken phrase {EXACT_AUDIO_ANCHOR}, the viewer needs to see {NEW_INFORMATION_OR_CAUSAL_STATE}.
DRAMATIC BEAT: {BEAT_ID_AND_PHASE; WHAT_THE_VIEWER_ANTICIPATES_OR_REALIZES; HOW_THIS_VIEW_SHOWS_THE_ACTIVE_PRESSURE_DISCOVERY_OR_CONSEQUENCE}.
REFERENCES: {ACTUAL_ORDERED_INPUT_PURPOSES: CURRENT_SETUP / SUBJECT_DESIGN / MATERIAL_DETAIL}. {EXACT_EDIT_IF_EDITING}.
SPACE: {VISIBLE_SUBJECT_COUNT_AND_SCALE; FOREGROUND_MIDGROUND_BACKGROUND; PERSISTENT_LANDMARK_RELATIONSHIPS; LIGHT_DIRECTION}.

VIEWPOINT INTENT: {AUDIENCE_RELATION_TO_SUBJECT; THREAT_ALIGNMENT_VULNERABILITY_OR_DIAGNOSTIC_PURPOSE; ONE_DOMINANT_DETAIL_AT_VIEWING_SIZE}.

MOTION READINESS: {SPACE_AND_VISIBLE_GEOMETRY_FOR_THE_SUBJECT_PATH; REQUIRED_CONTACT_OR_SUPPORT; DEPTH_LAYERS_FOR_CAMERA_PARALLAX}. Capture {PRECISE_OPENING_PHASE}, leaving the intended action available to perform rather than already completed. {IF_MECHANICALLY_CRITICAL: CLEAR_MOVING_PART_JOINT_AND_FIXED_REFERENCE; UNOBSTRUCTED_SWEEP_OR_TRAVEL_SPACE; VIEW_THAT_WILL_REVEAL_THE_REQUIRED_ENDPOINT}.
CONTINUITY: Carry forward {SPECIFIC_PREVIOUS_STATE}. Keep {ACTUAL_INVARIANTS}; show {ONLY_AUTHORIZED_CHANGE}. {FEW_SHOT_SPECIFIC_FAILURES_TO_AVOID}.
```

A still is one instant, not an instruction to perform several seconds of choreography. Select the useful starting phase instead of repeatedly commissioning generic hero poses. For mechanisms show the state/connection that explains operation. For acting show a meaningful task, thought or reaction. For environments give scale, layers and a reason for the camera to move.

Keep the production-input board distinct from the action/story review board. The former shows each selected starting image; the latter must let the creator see decisive action and outcome, not just a row of preparations. For important beats include an explicitly labelled planned contact/result state where useful, or actual decisive frames once video exists. A cut can begin during an ongoing action, at contact or in aftermath when continuity supports it; not every opening must precede all action. Never rewrite an input's lineage or present an extracted result frame as the original generation reference. A blank pre-fire input does not prove missing video action, and an attractive pre-fire board does not prove that the action works.

Give the video model useful visible evidence: a plausible grip and support, separation between interacting limbs/props, a readable travel path, enough room for the planned movement and stable background landmarks. Do not put both pre-action and post-action poses, motion trails, duplicate objects or diagram arrows into a normal frame. Keep indispensable identities and contacts inside the delivery crop. Generate a genuinely new viewpoint when the plan calls for one, not a tighter crop mislabeled as a reverse angle. Inspect the actual selected still before deriving video: an already released object, opened door or altered cast changes what can happen next.

Use the actual available GPT image tool with authorized local references. Do not silently replace it with Qwen, Higgsfield images or another billed API route. Review the output against the requested camera and state; changing the camera notes to describe a failed image is not a correction.

## MiniMax video template

### Cut coverage and camera motivation
For this creator's energetic film style, divide continuous scene audio into visual beats that show a developing event: an entrance, preparation, contact, an object changing hands or place, a reaction, or a result. Change the view when the viewer needs new information. A long scene is not one long shot by default. Several narrated events need coverage; a 14-second drift past one held pose is not that coverage. Short foreground beats often need about 2–5 seconds, while a longer shot must contain enough purposeful visual development to earn its duration. Do not impose equal lengths or a fixed montage formula on every subject.

Give each camera move a subject and a reason: follow the entering person, travel beside the carried object, move from the hand contact toward its result, arc enough to reveal an obscured detail, or track the event's path. Specify the camera's starting position, path, point of attention and ending view in the same fixed world. A closer crop alone is not a new viewpoint; a generic slow push or orbit is not a substitute for an event. Quiet reactions still need a thought, eyeline or task that changes. Preserve the selected low-poly or other rendering language rather than making every model input photorealistic.

Describe only subjects actually visible in the input. An unoccupied object/set insert does not need human anatomy, face or clothing clauses; state its empty subject count and rigid object supports instead. Distinguish the virtual viewpoint from any camera apparatus inside the set, so moving the viewpoint does not imply a new operator or moving the apparatus. Do not treat a correct upload or successful job as proof that the output honored this subject count.

Order the action phases before rendering. Keep movement and transfers in causal order; do not reset an event to an earlier state across a cut. Track identity, costume, location, direction, support, ownership, prop condition and set landmarks. A genuine time/travel ellipsis or explanatory callback may change state, but it must be deliberate and legible. Resolve the next prompt from the actual selected image, including whether a hand has already released an object; never invent a second grasp to reproduce a written plan.

For an entrance or exit, resolve the subject's current facing direction before describing travel. If the destination is behind them, include the turn, then the steps and the doorway occlusion that proves arrival or departure. A screen direction alone can leave the model walking past the destination or toward the viewer. Keep the viewpoint restrained when the turn, carried load and another person's doorway contact already make the event complex.

```text
LOOK AND INPUT: Detailed low-poly Blender-style cinematic 3D at {FPS}: preserve simplified modeled forms, faceted silhouettes, stylized cast, material response and lighting, without drifting to photographic realism or toy-like plastic. {PROJECT_DESIGN_LOCKS_OR_EXPLICIT_CREATOR_STYLE_OVERRIDE}. <Picture 1> fixes this shot's actual opening composition, subject designs, material and geometry. {ROLES_OF_ANY_ADDITIONAL_COMPATIBLE_INPUTS}.
START: {THE_ACCEPTED_IMAGE'S_REAL_ACTION_PHASE; INCOMING_VELOCITY; SUPPORT_OR_CONTACT; OWNED_PROPS_AND_GAZE}.
PHYSICAL JOB: {THIS_CUTS_BEAT_AND_VISIBLE_CHANGE; ACTION_SOURCE_AND_TARGET; PATH; RESISTANCE_OR_OBSTACLE; CONTACT_OR_MISS_AND_OBSERVABLE_RESPONSE}. {ACTUAL_SUPPORT_WEIGHT_TRANSFER_RECOIL_DISPLACEMENT_OR_MECHANICAL_OUTPUT_RELEVANT_TO_THIS_EVENT}.
ACTION GEOMETRY: {VISIBLE_SOURCE_POINT_AND_ACTUAL_AIM; INHERITED_TRAVEL_DIRECTION; INTENDED_CONTACT_POINT_AND_RESULT}. {CRITICAL_RELATION_VERIFIED_IN_THE_SELECTED_STILL}. Do not create a moving object beside its origin without travelling toward the established destination.

Complete the travel by {CUT_LOCAL_TIME}, then show {SETTLING_AND_READABLE_RESULT}. {PHYSICAL_ENDPOINT_EVIDENCE_AND_ONLY_THEN_NEXT_OPERATION}. CAMERA: One continuous {PHYSICAL_CAMERA_PATH_OR_OBJECT_ATTACHMENT_WITH_EXPLICIT_OFFSET}, beginning {START_VIEW}, aiming at {LOOK_AT_TARGET_OR_HANDOFF}, keeping {OPTICAL_FOCUS_TARGET} sharp, with {ROLL_AND_HORIZON_POLICY}, and ending {END_VIEW_TO_REVEAL_THE_RESULT_OR_JOIN}. {FOREGROUND_OCCLUSION_AND_DEPTH_DEPENDENT_PARALLAX; SCREEN_ATTENTION_FOR_THE_JOIN}. Distinguish camera travel from subject travel; keep the set fixed.
EVENT: Begin {PURPOSEFUL_MOVEMENT} on the first useful frames. {CAUSE_OR_LOADED_STATE} leads to {DECISIVE_ACTION_OR_MECHANISM}, then {READABLE_CONTACT_OR_CHANGE}, then {CONSEQUENCE_AND_RECOVERY_OR_CONTINUATION}. {FINITE_ACTION_COUNT_IF_REPETITION_IS_A_RISK}. Complete the useful beat within {MEASURED_SELECTED_CUT_DURATION_AND_LANDMARKS}, at natural event speed. Do not stretch it over the model's longer native duration or add an idle lead-in. Extra native frames continue the same outgoing state without restarting the action.
SYNC: {CUT_LOCAL_PHASE_WINDOWS_AND_REAL_SUPPLIED_AUDIO_ANCHORS_OR_NARRATION_ONLY_SEMANTIC_WINDOW}. Preparation precedes any fixed contact anchor. {PLANNED_EFFECTS_ADDED_AFTER_OBSERVED_CONTACT_OR_ACTUAL_SUPPORTED_AUDIO_REFERENCE_ROLE}. Timing language directs the shot; it is not a guarantee of frame-exact model control.
END STATE: End with {CONCRETE_OUTGOING_STATE_OF_THIS_SHOT_ONLY}. Preserve {SPECIFIC_SUBJECTS_PROPS_SCALE_AND_SET_RELATIONSHIPS}. {FOR_A_PAYOFF: ESTABLISHED_OBJECT_OR_CONTEXT; OBSERVABLE_ATTEMPT_CHANGE_AND_RESPONSE; WHAT_THE_VIEWER_CAN_READ_WITHOUT_AN_EXPLANATORY_LABEL}. New objects must enter from an established direction or occlusion.
SOUND: {EXACT_EFFECTS_AND_AMBIENCE_OR_SILENT}. Narration is the separately approved Higgsfield file, mixed locally: no generated narration, dialogue or music in this shot unless a specifically authorized native performance requires it.
CONSTRAINTS: One uninterrupted shot, no extra camera cuts, dissolve, background morph, new event or frozen lead-in. {ONLY_OBSERVED_OR_RELEVANT_LOCAL_RISKS}.
```

Physical direction depends on the event. A strike needs loaded balance, acceleration, visible contact, target displacement and recovery; dust and shake cannot substitute for them. Running needs push-off/flight/landing, counter-motion and equipment lag. A machine needs an actuator, linkage, resistance and output. Quiet acting needs a thought, eyeline or purposeful task; do not append combat recoil to a conversation. Camera movement needs motivation and a changing view, not an orbit on every shot.

For mechanical completion resolve the optional template line into plain physical language, using only relevant measurements. Show the part's final relation to its body/support, not merely a dramatic camera orbit. Check that the selected starting image leaves the intended travel possible. Verify completion in the actual retained frames before accepting a following operation. A prompt saying 'fully' or a successful generation receipt is insufficient. For a decisive fast action, choose a viewpoint and attention target that expose the operation and its effect; split coverage where one view cannot communicate both, preserving action count and avoiding replay.

Smooth camera movement must not turn a quick event into slow motion. State the intended action speed separately from camera speed and name the moving attention target. A following camera may match the subject's travel; an orbit moves relative to it; an attached view shares its motion. In each case the background stays fixed and produces depth-dependent parallax rather than sliding or warping as a flat plate. Preserve planted support, weight transfer, momentum, resistance and recovery even with stylized geometry. For reactions specify the observable behavior, not an abstract instruction to feel an emotion. Keep each prompt focused enough for one short continuous take; a new location, reverse, impact insert or time jump belongs in the edit unless the planned physical camera move actually connects it.

Keep the following cut's event and props in the editorial plan, outside the generation prompt. Resolve continuity by specifying this shot's outgoing pose, ownership and set state. Telling the model that a later shot contains writing, an arrest or a new location can invite that event into the current continuous shot. This is a prompting risk, not proof of the cause of any particular failure. A static object insert should state its rigid state throughout; do not attach an upcoming human action to its ending.

## References and cut boundaries
Use the minimum sufficient compatible inputs. A second whole-scene image with changed geometry can make a model morph the world; more references are not automatically more continuity. Compare perspective, horizon, scale, landmarks, lighting and action chronology. Use another input as a design/detail reference only when that is its actual purpose. Do not force incompatible angles into start/end constraints. A real cut gets a separate generated video and retains continuous scene audio.

Scope reference roles explicitly. A face/prop reference for a new location must not import its old room or other cast. A rendering-style reference must not import its composition. A frame with the object absent must not override a later restored-object state. Prefer one selected opening image for each continuous video when it already contains the required designs and geography. A camera change across editorial cuts does not require interpolation between two incompatible stills.

## Timing, review and costs
Read the installed MiniMax instructions and live config. Use native frame count and actual duration, not a guessed padding rule. Keep the scene narration at its accepted pace. Cover it with motivated shots; do not stretch an attack or loop a performance to fill speech. If tool duration limits conflict with a planned shot, adjust shot coverage deliberately before generation instead of cutting words or secretly creating a montage inside the take.

After a scene's images are selected and registered, place them immediately as held unapproved previews with phrase-based cut points. Preserve originals and alternatives. Once video is completed, check opening/middle/end geometry, action/contact/consequence, persistent state and adjacent joins. Inspect at native rate when available before claiming natural timing or force. Frame sheets and probes support narrower technical/visual findings only. Keep unavailable listening/playback approval pending. Save actual paid calls once, credits separately from unknown GCP infrastructure cost, and allow the installed shared idle coordinator to unload model weights after all submitted jobs finish.
