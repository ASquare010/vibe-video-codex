# Benchmark proposal: Lightning Hit This Moon Rocket—Twice

Prepared 2026-10-31. Status: researched recommendation and evaluation plan; no new film, audio or images generated. Timing clarification checkpoint: `b0495a1`.

## Recommendation

Make an approximately 90–120-second cinematic 3D historical suspense explainer about Apollo 12's lightning-struck launch and the recovery of its instrument data. Working YouTube title: **Lightning Hit This Moon Rocket—Twice**. This is a creative/production benchmark recommendation, not a claim of measured search demand or likely views.

The attraction is the contrast between a huge emergency and a tiny consequential operation. The audience should finish understanding what the switch changed and why the mission could continue. That directly challenges the earlier failure mode of exciting-looking images accompanying an unclear or contradictory explanation.

This exercises the complete staged workflow with a contained event and a small number of recurring environments. It does not test every genre or every physical action: running, carrying, fighting and door mechanics would need another benchmark. Do not invent those events to increase coverage here.

> Write an engaging, understandable historical story about Apollo 12 being struck by lightning twice during launch and how its crew and Mission Control recovered. Useful developments to assess in its draft are the emergency, the difficulty reading the spacecraft's condition, recognition of a possible fix, the crew's action and the resulting recovery. These are story developments, not prescribed scenes, cuts or exact spoken sentences.

## Research and factual boundaries

- Apollo 12 launched on November 14, 1969. Lightning struck about 36 seconds and again 52 seconds after liftoff. Use these as mission elapsed times, not automatic film timestamps. [NASA: Repeat Performance](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/repeat-performance-apollo-12-achieves-second-moon-landing/).
- John Aaron recognized the garbled data pattern; the recommendation passed through Mission Control to the crew. Alan Bean moved the SCE switch to Auxiliary and usable data returned. Define SCE in plain spoken language rather than assuming viewers understand the abbreviation. [NASA: Return to the Moon](https://www.nasa.gov/history/50-years-ago-return-to-the-moon/).
- Restoring telemetry and resetting the fuel cells were separate steps, followed by further checks. Do not make the SCE movement restart the rocket engines or instantly repair every system. [NASA: Apollo Expeditions to the Moon, chapter 7.3](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-350/ch-7-3.html).

Use those distinctions to check the actual script and edit. Do not add an invented imminent explosion, abort countdown, dialogue, switch design or action for suspense. Before detailed instrument images, obtain a relevant Apollo panel/manual reference and verify the control's location, travel and positions; those precise visual details have not been verified by this idea-selection pass. Historical photos on the NASA pages can ground appearance, but photographs alone do not establish the entire switch operation.

The old NASA Flight Journal address redirects to NASA's journal portal. Its currently linked detailed essay is at [Apollo Journals](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap12fj/a12-lightningstrike.html); use its transcript/document links for deeper follow-up, while tracing factual claims to the original records. The NTRS PDF download for *What Made Apollo a Success?* timed out during this pass; it was not treated as a fully read source.

## How to run a fair test

1. Save the current skill snapshot and minimal brief in a new project. Existing film projects retain their frozen instructions and accepted media.
2. Accept the spoken story on its own merits before narration and visual planning. Do not fix a missing explanation only in director notes.
3. Establish a measured and listened-to native narration pilot, then generate scene audio and lock the accepted files. Derive exact cut timing from those files, not the proposed runtime or a fixed number of words per cut.
4. Make the director's timed board and inspect the selected stills. Exercise the essential hand/control/result sequence before scaling animation. Preserve the first attempts, prompts, references and findings, including failures.
5. Generate and edit through the staged workflow. Compare actual retained footage with exact speech and neighboring states; make bounded repairs for demonstrated defects using the existing skill's procedure.
6. Have the director assess the finished export and save the worksheet below. Keep listening or motion judgments pending if the permitted review route is unavailable. Do not infer them from contact sheets or successful decode.

This test should not quietly receive new reusable rules halfway through. Record any project-specific repair and any proposed skill revision separately, so a visually successful repaired film does not conceal a weak first pass.

## Evaluation worksheet

For each capability above, record **not checked**, **0: failed**, **1: partial**, or **2: clear**, plus the relevant scene/cut/version, final time interval and observed evidence. This is a proposed local rubric, not a validated audience score. Preserve both first-pass and final findings.

Critical failures cannot be averaged away by attractive visuals: a wrong historical explanation, narration–action contradiction, missing/reversed essential operation, broken causal continuity, or unavailable final listening/motion review prevents a complete pass. Pending review is distinct from a demonstrated failure and from creator approval.

Record actual generated/accepted/rejected takes, first-pass usable cuts, repair reasons, native/selected durations, speed changes and known provider charges. Count actual jobs once; leave unpriced infrastructure cost unknown. A before/after quality claim requires a comparable baseline—this first film establishes one, rather than proving improvement by itself.

After viewing, ask a first-time viewer: What went wrong? What did the switch change? What else happened before the recovery was confirmed? Where was anything confusing or unnaturally slow? Their answers test comprehension and perceived rhythm; do not invent viewer feedback or retention data.
