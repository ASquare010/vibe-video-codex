# Apollo 12 benchmark: parent-chat review

## Scope and verdict

Reviewed the complete saved 297-word script, all 27 selected stills, 92 unique sampled frames from the delivered 93.125-second export, denser selected-range samples through the SCE action, relevant director/prompt records, and source-versus-export frames at ten suspect boundaries. Project: `34dd7600-10be-483d-8b19-4bc92f818408`, revision 71. Export SHA-256: `268c1be93d2ade770f97737f5369bbfda57295c0738f12b1ff2fe6aae6ded12d`.

This is a direct inspection by the parent chat, with access to the production report; it is not a blind review or viewer feedback. Native playback and listening were not performed. Motion smoothness, felt speech delivery, exact spoken-word fidelity and phrase-level synchronization remain pending. Ordered frames establish sampled states and some visible changes, not the complete viewing experience. The project, film, accepted media and reusable skill were left unchanged.

**Verdict:** the story has a coherent causal structure and the stills have a recognizable visual style. The main creative weakness is communication of change: many pictures establish the correct subject without making the specific discovery, operation or consequence obvious. A separate export defect adds black frames. Neither more shot count nor a longer camera-angle list directly addresses these failures.

## What works

- The opening begins with a concrete incident instead of announcing a topic or exercise.
- The script distinguishes restored telemetry, separate fuel-cell recovery and later orbital checks. Preserve these distinctions in any revision.
- The detailed low-poly look, warm cabin lighting, green CRTs and recurring environments give the film a coherent identity in the inspected stills.
- The S04-B/C/D coverage progressively isolates a meaningful hand/control action. The selected S04-D endpoint is visibly lower than the opening position in the sampled final frames. It is a useful starting point, despite the timing and continuity limitations below.
- Completed assets and original attempts are preserved. The actual local film exists, and the selected source ranges are recorded. Those are valuable production properties, distinct from creative acceptance.

## Findings in priority order

### 1. Fix the black-frame assembly defect first

**Confirmed output defect.** The final export contains ten near-black single frames, each 1/24 second, at 3.708, 6.417, 15.375, 26.292, 55.125, 60.417, 73.083, 76.083, 87.958 and 89.958 seconds. One retains a small text overlay; the picture underneath is black. These are not scene transitions saved in the project: its transition list is empty.

For every affected boundary, the corresponding selected native source frame contains a normal image and the next exported frame contains a normal image. This localizes the problem to assembly/export rather than a black opening generated by MiniMax. Exact filter/time-base cause is not yet established. See [the measured comparison](../../evaluations/apollo-12-parent-review/black-frame-source-comparison.json).

**Improvement:** correct the exporter in a separate, focused change, then verify the same film without regenerating media or changing narration. Add a real export check for unexpected black frames at covered visual boundaries. Successful decode and gap-free clip arithmetic did not detect this failure. Do not turn it into another prompt rule.

### 2. Make the central recovery visually distinguishable

**Strong frame-based communication finding.** S01-C (6.417–10.292 seconds) represents garbled telemetry; S04-E (57.125–60.417 seconds) represents usable telemetry. Both show dense, reasonably organized green columns on a CRT beside a controller. At the inspected viewing size the difference is weak; the later subtitle, “USABLE TELEMETRY RETURNS,” supplies a result that the image does not clearly demonstrate by itself.

![Selected exported frames before and after recovery](D:/Git/vibe-video-codex/evaluations/apollo-12-parent-review/telemetry-before-after.jpg)

**Improvement:** the director should define an observable before/after contrast for the important consequence, and the image role should stage a view in which that contrast is readable. Keep the relevant screen geometry consistent. Show an interpretable distinction between disrupted information and stable usable readings, followed by the controller checking it. Verify any historically specific display behavior against references; a controlled explanatory graphic can clarify the relationship without fabricating exact historical values. A caption can reinforce the evidence, but should not be its sole substitute.

This does not require every sentence to be illustrated literally. Context can accompany speech. The load-bearing action and consequence need stronger evidence than an ordinary contextual cut.

### 3. Reject redundant coverage at the image-board stage

**Confirmed composition repetition; editorial judgment about its effect.** S03-A, S03-D, S04-A and S04-E repeatedly place Aaron, pencil, checklist and CRT in similar console-relative compositions. The prompt records describe different intentions and viewpoints, but the selected images converge. Their combined selected duration is about 14.3 seconds. S01-C, S02-E and S05-D add further console coverage.

There is no rule that each of these must have a wildly different angle. The issue is that recognition, uncertainty, recommendation and recovery look too similar. Labels in the plan such as “specific recognition” do not make a generic looking-at-screen image specific.

**Improvement:** inspect adjacent stills together before animation. State what new fact or audience response each view adds. Where coverage repeats, the director can consolidate or redirect its dramatic job, and the image role can choose an appropriately different attention target, scale, relationship or perspective. Preserve necessary geography. Correct an actual failed camera request rather than rewriting its description to accept the failure. Avoid imposing a fixed shot-size rotation as another formula.

### 4. Shape the brief action around its complete motion and result

**Selected-frame finding; native cadence remains unreviewed.** S04-B/C prepare the control over 51.000–55.125 seconds. S04-D then occupies 55.125–57.125 seconds. Dense samples show the lever still raised through approximately 56.625 seconds; a lower position appears around 56.792 seconds, close to the end of the cut. Much of the selected macro interval is approach/contact preparation, with relatively little exposed result. The first frame of this cut is also affected by the export defect.

**Improvement:** consider the complete B/C/D sequence when selecting the action phase. Avoid spending repeated views on hovering and reaching. Give the actual movement enough time to be read, then show its endpoint clearly for the duration that this specific action needs. Choose useful source in/out and only then a justified speed change if needed. A cut of 0.8, 2 or another number of seconds is not inherently correct. Speeding up an obscured operation would not make its completion clearer.

S04-F (60.417–64.375 seconds) does show a separate reach toward upper controls, but the tiny reset and the hand's occlusion make completion difficult to inspect. Improve its visibility and achieved endpoint without merging it with the SCE operation or inventing an exact procedure.

### 5. Match the visual phase of an event, not just its noun

**Potential semantic mismatch, stated as a visual reading.** The opening words place the first strike 36.5 seconds after liftoff. S01-A's selected still and opening exported frames read as a rocket lifting away from the pad, with nearby ground and the engine plume rooted close to it. NASA describes the first incident at about 6,600 feet altitude and in cloud. The picture therefore risks communicating a much earlier phase of ascent. Camera perspective alone does not let this review measure its exact altitude. [NASA's launch account](https://www.nasa.gov/history/50-years-ago-return-to-the-moon/).

**Improvement:** put the meaningful phase into the directed incoming state—already airborne in cloud, with the appropriate surrounding geography—and verify the actual image against it. “Rocket ascending” was too broad to catch this distinction. This transfers to doors, keys, weapons and vehicles: the right object performing a vaguely related motion can still depict the wrong event.

### 6. Use explanatory screen time to reveal a relationship

**Confirmed content allocation; editorial judgment.** S03-C holds an electronics box and connectors for 5.917 seconds, the film's longest cut. The sampled footage adds bright cable highlights, but does not visibly explain how sensor information becomes unusable or why an auxiliary setting helps. The animation prompt explicitly treats it as stationary context. An attractive hardware view is valid context; it is weak coverage for this much of the explanation.

**Improvement:** either shorten the contextual view or develop an accurate, simple visual explanation across the allowed scene coverage. Let viewers follow the relevant information path and its disruption, then the recovery. This belongs in the director's choice of observable facts; the image role decides how to make them legible. Adding a camera orbit or brighter glowing cables would not supply the missing relationship.

The script can also make the stakes of the technical explanation more conversational. “The equipment that prepares instrument readings for transmission” is accurate but dense on first hearing. Name the sensor-to-Houston relationship in plain language and connect the bad information to the crew's real power problem. Do not imply all warnings were false: the fuel cells really had disconnected.

### 7. Tighten the script's later movement and payoff

**Editorial improvement, not an established factual error.** The opener is the strongest writing. At the start of scene two, “It is November fourteenth, nineteen sixty-nine” briefly retreats into introduction after the emergency is already underway. The date can be integrated more economically or left to a useful contextual overlay. Preserve the first and second strikes as distinct events.

The orbital section repeats its point through “getting there is only the first test,” the system-check sentence, “They need a working ship,” and the next scene's “The checks are good enough to continue.” Keep the factual checks and their consequence; remove explanatory restatement that adds no development. “Good enough” is an especially weak emotional release after a specific technical recovery.

At 84.458–93.125 seconds, pictures go Moon surface → Earth return → Moon surface. This is labelled a retrospective outcome in the plan, so it is not automatically a historical contradiction. It repeats a visual payoff and interrupts an otherwise chronological closure. A cleaner ending can progress through lunar success to the crew's return, or choose one deliberate final image and write toward it.

### 8. Separate provider artifacts and quality settings from direction errors

**Sampled output artifacts.** S01-A's later sampled cloud/sky bands become bright and wavy; S03-B's CRT gains colored edge glow; S03-C's cables brighten; S05-A develops a conspicuous rainbow-like flare. Those effects compete with a restrained historical reconstruction. They should not be accepted merely because cast count and camera labels are correct.

The export is 1280×720, while most native video is 864×480. Checked requests including S01-A, S03-C and selected S04-D use turbo with four steps. This is a low-resolution baseline, not evidence that those settings caused each artifact. A saved 20-step repair was not automatically better according to the production record.

**Improvement:** after the semantic and timing problems are fixed, compare a small difficult sequence at a higher supported native quality using the same selected opening images and prompts. Record the changed variables, cost and observed result. Do not regenerate the whole film on the assumption that resolution or steps will fix direction.

## Narration and sound: what can and cannot be concluded

The saved script has 297 whitespace-delimited words over 93.125 seconds: a nominal 191 words per minute including pauses. That supports checking delivery at the existing native rate before requesting another global speed increase. It does not establish actual spoken accuracy, expressiveness, pronunciation or whether this pace feels comfortable.

The production record reports voice-only assembly with native video audio muted and no added effects/music. A restrained warning cue, room/vehicle ambience and a well-timed switch click are sound-design opportunities. They were not auditioned here, and a click cannot certify missing visible contact. Listen to the voice and final mix through a permitted route before adjusting rate or pause rules. Check actual phrase timing before claiming synchronization or contradiction at a precise word boundary.

## What the benchmark says about the workflow

The roles and ownership help preserve the written event sequence. Having a single director does not guarantee the generated picture implements that sequence. Several relevant instructions already existed, including camera variety, action completion and actual-output checks; this film exposes acceptance/execution gaps as well as a few under-specified visible states.

| Owner | Next improvement |
| --- | --- |
| Application/export | Frame-accurate boundaries and inspection for unintended black output, separate from manifest coverage and decode. |
| Writer | Preserve the strong opening, simplify load-bearing jargon and remove later restatement; keep visuals out of the writer's remit. |
| Director | Define perceptible causal changes and useful information per cut; inspect the whole sequence's preparation/action/result and exact event phase. |
| Image prompt writer | Make critical details readable and inspect actual viewpoint/adjacent-board differences before animation. |
| Animation prompt writer and selection | Favor a completed useful operation with a visible result; reject unexplained exposure/glow changes and keep actual state distinct from prompt intent. |
| Director's final review | Inspect the delivered export, not only native takes or a successful job ledger. Keep unavailable listening/motion checks pending. |

Recommended sequence: repair the export without new media; then test the existing audio's recovery sequence around S04-B–F with clearer before/after evidence, source selection and coverage. Preserve this baseline. Only afterward decide which transferable decisions need reusable-template changes. Avoid appending a separate rule for each Apollo-specific artifact or baking in a fixed insert duration.
